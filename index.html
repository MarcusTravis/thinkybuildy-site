In plain English

What ThinkyBuildy is, and who it's for.

What it is

ThinkyBuildy is a set of eleven free websites that each answer one kind of question: how many days until a date, whether bigger tires will throw off your speedometer, how many grams are in a cup of flour, where the big power outages are right now, or how two well pumps or fall protection harnesses compare.

Who it's for

Anyone who wants a quick, straight answer without wading through pop-ups: home cooks, drivers, homeowners on well water, hunters and shooters, people building an emergency kit, safety and construction folks, BIM and VDC teams, and anyone trying to keep up with AI models and tools.

Why free and ad-free

A lot of simple calculators online are buried under banner ads, autoplay videos and "sign up to see your result" walls. ThinkyBuildy tools skip all of that. They're static pages that load fast, work on a phone, and never ask for an email.

How it stays honest

AI agents do the research and building, but the reference tools cite their sources, the math is re-checked before release, and a human approves every change. Optional tips on Ko-fi help keep the lights on.

FAQ

Common questions

What is ThinkyBuildy?

ThinkyBuildy is a small collection of free, ad-free websites: calculators, charts and reference guides. Each one does one job, like comparing tire sizes, converting cups to grams or showing major power outages on a map. A crew of AI agents does the research and building, and a human reviews every release before it goes live.

Are the tools really free? What's the catch?

Yes, they're free. There are no ads, no premium tier, no trial and no locked results. If a tool helps you, you can leave an optional tip on Ko-fi, but nothing on any tool depends on it.

Do I need to sign up or make an account?

No. There are no accounts and no email gates. Open the page, use the tool, and bookmark it if you want to come back.

How are the tools built?

Each tool goes through the same steps. One AI agent scouts what people actually need and checks we're not copying anyone, one builds the site, one re-checks the math and the sources, and a human tests it on a phone and approves the release.

Where does the data come from?

The reference tools cite their sources. For example, Cups & Quarts uses USDA ingredient data, the AI Model Chart uses OpenRouter, Epoch AI and LMArena figures, and the reference libraries cite their ratings. The Live Power Outage Map is unofficial and links the reports behind each outage.

Can I suggest a tool or report a mistake?

Yes. Email hello@thinkybuildy.com with the tool you'd like to see or the mistake you found.
